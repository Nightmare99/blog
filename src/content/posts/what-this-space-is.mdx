The portfolio this blog hangs off of renders every section as a route being inspected — nodes, edges, status codes. This is the same idea pointed at time instead of navigation: a log. Not a "tech blog" in the narrow sense, even though a lot of what ends up here will be technical.

## What goes in the log

Four kinds of entries, color-coded on the index so you can tell at a glance what you're about to read:

- **engineering** — write-ups on things I built or broke. Postmortems, notes on a design decision, the occasional "here's how a piece of this site actually works."
- **career** — reflections on projects and roles. What a project taught me, decisions I'd make differently, patterns that generalized beyond the specific job.
- **notes** — shorter, lower-stakes entries. Observations, things I learned that don't need a full write-up. This entry is one of those.
- **personal** — updates, feelings, memories. Not everything worth writing down is technical.

That last category is the reason this isn't just a portfolio appendix. A changelog for a person includes more than shipped features.

## Why log format

No comment sections, no algorithmic feed, no engagement mechanics — just entries in order, each one timestamped and tagged, readable start to finish or skimmed by category. The oldest entry is `#001`; whatever's newest sits at the top. Nothing here is trying to perform virality; it's closer to a running record I'd want to be able to look back on.

## Cadence

Whenever there's something worth writing down — no fixed schedule, no content calendar. Some entries will be a few paragraphs after shipping something; some will be shorter than this one.

If you're here from the portfolio: welcome to the log.
